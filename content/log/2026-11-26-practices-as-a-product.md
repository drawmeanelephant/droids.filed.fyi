# Practices as a product

- Surface: industry — what Factory actually sells
- Date: 2026-11-26
- Evidence: a month of receipts, the acquisition record, and one
  evening spent measuring what the subscription left on disk
- Verdict: keep — the frame the whole retrospective runs on

The sharpest description I found for the product arrived while I was
cleaning out the disk: Factory is practices as a product. It sells
best practices — contract-shaped work, acceptance gates, evidence
trails, K-of-N repetition — wrapped around metered pass-through of
several vendors' inference, sometimes well.

The trouble is that the practices were already mine. The repos had
card-shaped issues, acceptance bars, readiness checks, and claim
ledgers before the subscription started. The part of Factory that
genuinely improved outcomes was the part I was not really buying.

The acquisition record says the company knows exactly what it is
selling. HumanLayer's claim to fame is a viral prompt framework —
Research, Plan, Implement. Factory did not just sell practices as a
product; in November it acquired the practices-content company
outright. Mentlio exists because token-spend measurement is a real
hole. Each acquisition is a map of a gap the product cannot fill
organically.

## The physical form of the thing

You can see the product's shape on disk. Every ephemeral workspace
the harness touches gets its own copy of the scaffolding: a
`.factory` directory with the skills, the orientation document, the
claims protocol — the job jacket, photocopied into every temp tree.
In `/private/tmp` right now there are nine retry worktrees for a
single capture-vector PR, each one a full Rust workspace of eight to
eleven gigabytes, one hundred gigabytes of temp trees in total. The
discipline is real — the jacket is genuinely good — but the economics
of photocopying it into every ephemeral workspace and never cleaning
up are the economics of the whole product: the structure is yours to
keep, and so is the bill.

## Who it is for

Practices as a product is worth the most to people who lack the
practices — teams with no contract culture, no gates, no evidence
discipline, where the scaffolding is genuinely new. It is worth the
least to people who already run their work that way, because the
differentiating layer is a codified version of discipline they
already own, and what remains is inference routing with a meter. The
marketing cannot say this, because "our product mostly benefits
people who have not yet built a working relationship with rigor"
does not close deals. But it is the honest segmentation, and it
predicts exactly who will love the product and who will spend a
month paying to rent their own discipline back.
